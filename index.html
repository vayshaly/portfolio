The page says 19 of 24 participants improved. Your campaign proposal says 80. Your emails have said 57 and 18. I am buying a measurement product. Our platform marked a lesson complete when someone clicked a button. The video ran through a separate service that recorded what was actually played. Nobody had joined the two, because there was no reason to expect them to disagree.

They disagree. One participant had marked two lessons complete against six minutes of video watched. Another had marked nothing and watched fifty-nine. A third had marked more lessons done than anyone in the cohort with no playback recorded at all.

Each has an innocent explanation. The point is not that anyone is suspicious. The point is that the two measures do not track each other, and we had been reporting one as though it meant the other. Completion is no longer quoted as evidence of learning.

The email carried one call to action and it went straight to a purchase page. For someone who had heard of us ten seconds earlier there was no step between interest and paying. We replaced it with a free first day and changed nothing else of substance.

The team wanted to scale a target segment. I read all 71 contacts in it first and found 25 explicit declines and no genuine buying interest in the rest.

My recommendation was to abandon the segment rather than put more volume through it, which was not what anyone wanted to hear. It was dropped and the list rebuilt. Knowing when to stop is a cheaper skill than knowing how to push harder, and far rarer.

A ten video series was going nowhere. When I pulled the traffic sources, 67 percent of views came from inside the playlist itself, which means almost nobody arrived from outside. Two episodes had a click-through rate of 0.0 percent: YouTube showed them to people and not one person clicked.

One video broke the pattern at 7.89 percent. It was the only one in the series not titled by its episode number. It was titled as the thing you would walk away with.

The series was never failing on content. Every thumbnail was saying "Day 7" to someone who had never heard of day 1.
